Orbit is no longer empty space.

Low Earth orbit is already crowded with countless communication satellites. Some belong to giant state-led projects, others to ambitious ventures backed by private capital. Over limited orbital slots and limited frequency bands, nations and companies around the world were locked in a quiet but fierce competition.

Then an international agreement was born: PROJECT EDGEWORTH, a communication infrastructure project shared by the whole world and owned by no single nation or company. Under the leadership of the international body OCC (Orbital Communications Council), organizations selected from around the world take part, each building and proving its own satellite network and competing on the results.

The rules are simple. Launching satellites alone means nothing. You must put the right people in the field, choose equipment you can trust, and build robustness into every line of software. Then you must run real communication checks at sites all over the world, from ground stations in the Arctic Circle to relay stations in deserts on the equator, and show results no one can dispute.

Only by building up those results can you meet the international certification standards set by PROJECT EDGEWORTH, and only that way can you win the trust and fame of the world.

The organizations taking part in PROJECT EDGEWORTH include giant corporations with the backing of nations and startups that rose from obscurity. Neither the size of their capital nor the strength of their political power makes a decisive difference here.

Only one question matters: how precisely and how reliably can you run things in the field? You are the commander leading one of these organizations. With precision and execution in the field, become the master of orbit.
